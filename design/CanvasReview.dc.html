Stormalong Cider · Review of the canvas
Directions A–D, the top-of-page set, and what is missing

Reviewing the redesign
you already had

The earlier sheet reviewed the site. This one reviews the canvas, the four directions, the top-of-page options and the home page built out in each. The design work is good and the research-then-design chain behind Direction D is the strongest thing here. What follows is what I would fix before any of it reaches a build, plus what was simply absent until now. Checked against the live range as well as the repository.

Wrong on the facts 4 findings · fix before anyone quotes them
01

02

The trophy case undercounts its own best result

The panel reads “Two Best of Class”. There are three in the data: Mass Appeal and Kingston Black both took BEST OF CLASS at the LA Invitational, and Blue Skies took GLINTCAP Best in Class in 2021. The headline figure of 26 medals across 8 ciders is correct, I recounted it, and so is the 2015–2024 range. Only the Best of Class line is short, and it is short in the direction that undersells you.

04

The feature icons are on almost everything, and their labels are wrong

Every cider carries a row of little icons, and they are a genuinely good idea, they are the fastest thing on the page to read. As set up, they say nothing.

A badge on every product distinguishes nothing, so the two apple glyphs are decoration. Worse, the tooltip text is wrong: no cider in the data lists Gold Rush or Red Delicious in its blend, Legendary Dry names fifteen varieties and neither is among them. Those two labels are inaccurate on all eighteen products.

The flags that do vary, barrel-aged, Rare Apple Series, and the hibiscus, passionfruit and guava infusions, are the ones worth drawing. And the labels belong on screen, not in a tooltip: the current ones only appear on hover, which does not exist on a phone. Three more icon assets sit unused in public/images/icons/, gluten free, no added sugar, not from concentrate, which are real shelf claims with no data field behind them yet.

Gaps 2 findings · the canvas stops before the site does
05

The masthead promised four destinations and designed one

Every artboard carries the same nav, Ciders, Releases, Taproom, The Club, plus a “Find a can near you” button. Until now the canvas contained a design for the home page and nothing else, and the live app has routes for only two of those four. Releases, Taproom and the locator existed as promises in a navigation bar and nowhere else.

That is what the eight new page artboards on this canvas are for. One correction to my own earlier note, though: the locator is not missing from the business, stormalong.com/locator is live and working, running an embedded distributor finder, and every “Find It” on the live cider pages already reaches it. What 404s is the rebuild’s own /locator and /shop. The dead calls to action are a regression the redesign introduced, not a gap to fill from scratch, which makes that page the cheapest win here, since the tool and the data already exist. /shop is a different matter: it is an empty stub on the live site too, so either it points at the club or it leaves the navigation.

06

There is no footer anywhere in the canvas

Direction D ends on the taproom strip and stops. A footer is where three of the earlier findings actually get resolved: the newsletter that currently collects addresses and discards them, the “drink responsibly” line an alcohol brand is expected to carry, and the legal chrome. I have drawn one and used it on all eight new pages, so it is now a proposal you can react to rather than an omission.

Worth a decision 3 findings · not errors, but nobody has chosen
07

The canvas does not say which direction is live

Page 2 records that B was chosen and that A and C are kept “for the record, not for further work”. Page 3 then introduces D, and the canvas opens on it. Both are true, D is B’s voice on a new spine, but someone opening this cold cannot tell which one the business is building. A one-line note on page 2 pointing at D would settle it permanently.

08

The age gate is drawn but not priced

D opens with one, and the research is right that the category universally gates. The design is a genuine improvement on the plugin modal everyone else ships. What is not addressed is the cost: an interstitial sits between every inbound link and the page it promised, including paid social and search results, unless it is cookie’d and deliberately bot-aware. That is a compliance and acquisition decision, not a design one, and it should be made by whoever owns both.

09

Colour-blocking holds at fifteen cans, but only with a value ladder

D shows six colour-blocked tiles and it works. I sampled all fifteen current labels off the live site to see whether it scales. The dominant hues collapse into five families, not fifteen colours: red-orange at 10–13°, a crowded cream-gold band at 44–65° holding six cans, green-teal at 148–180°, deep navy at 205–218°, and one near-neutral. Legendary Dry and Mass Appeal sit 3° apart at almost identical luminance, they are simply both navy cans.

A straight eyedropper therefore produces eleven pairs of tiles that read the same. Taking the hue from the label but separating within each family by value fixes it: all fifteen then clear 4.5:1 against the cream type, the lowest is 5.44:1, and only two pairs still read alike, both in different groups where the headings keep them apart.

So the idea survives the full range. It just is not an eyedropper, it is a per-family value ladder, and it is worth writing down as a rule before someone builds it. The new Ciders artboard uses exactly this palette.

Checked against the live site what that changed

There is no taproom anywhere on stormalong.com

No taproom, no tasting room, no opening hours, no public address and no phone number appear anywhere on the live site. The Taproom page in this set was built from a category convention and an unused photograph in the repository, so it is now marked as an unverified proposal. Direction D’s masthead also lists Taproom as a destination. If there is no room open to the public, both should go.

The contact page offers one address, not three

The live page gives a single address, info@stormalong.com, for general, press, marketing and sales, plus the line about replying within 48 hours. An earlier version of the Contact artboard split it into separate trade, press and general routes. That was invention, and it has been removed.

Two product claims turned out to be documented

The “We Respect the apple” block on the home page states that the ciders never use concentrates and are all naturally gluten-free. Those were carried as unconfirmed on the detail artboard and can now be shown plainly.

The copy across this set was in my voice, not yours

Tasting notes, section headings and body copy on the first pass were written from scratch. They now come from your own pages: every tagline and flavour line from the individual cider pages, the founding story, the Sherborn mill history and Captain Stormalong from Our Story, the full membership terms from the Cider Club page, and the Rare Apple Series description from the cider pages. Em dashes have been removed throughout, along with the jokes.

Keep already load-bearing
✓

The research sheet driving Direction D. Seven structural conventions observed across four competitors, each one traced to a specific change in the design. That chain is the reason D is defensible and it is rare to see it written down.

✓

The trophy case. Twenty-six medals counted from the data rather than invented, on a brand that currently buries them in bullet lists. It barely changes year to year, so it earns a permanent slot without becoming a maintenance job.

✓

Three flavours of locator, take it home, drink it there, come to us. That is the correct decomposition for a distribution-led business and it is better than a single map.

✓

The bracketed placeholder discipline. Every unverified number on every artboard is marked. It is the difference between a mockup you can hand to a stakeholder and one that quietly invents facts.

✓

Gold on navy only. The contrast rule from the earlier review is actually honoured throughout D, including on the new pages.
