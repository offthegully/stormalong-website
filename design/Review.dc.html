Stormalong Cider · Design Review
stormalong.offthegully.com vs stormalong.com

What to change
in the current redesign

The redesign is a real improvement on the live site in typography, spacing and product information architecture. The problems are not aesthetic — they are that the site currently cannot sell anything, and that a handful of colour and contrast decisions undercut the work. Fourteen findings, in the order I would fix them.

Blockers 3 findings · ship-stoppers
01

Every commercial call to action is dead

On the cider detail pages, Find It points at /locator and Buy Now points at /shop. Neither route exists. Five routes are linked from the chrome and all five return 404:

/locator — nav “Find Us” /shop /store2 /press /blog

Finding a can nearby is the single most valuable thing this site does for a cider drinker, and it is the one thing it cannot do. Build the locator, or point Find Us and Buy Now at whatever the business already uses, until it exists.

02

Eight images are hotlinked to a dead scaffolding CDN

Six images in the Cider Club gallery and two on Our Story load from web-assets.same.dev, which returns 404 for all of them. Those sections render as broken-image icons today. Move the files into public/images/, then drop both scaffolding hosts from the remotePatterns allowlist in next.config.mjs so this cannot silently come back.

03

The image fallback is itself a broken image

Seven components fall back to /placeholder.svg when a cider has no artwork, and that file is not in public/. Add a real placeholder — a navy tile with the Stormalong mark would do — so a missing can reads as intentional rather than broken.

High 4 findings · visible to every visitor
04

Brand gold fails contrast on white — 2.1:1

The gold is doing two different jobs and only one of them works. On navy it is a comfortable 6.8:1. On white it is 2.1:1, well under the 4.5:1 floor — and that is exactly where it is used most: the cider detail headline, the apple varietal list, the awards list and the .cider-heading class.

Legendary Dry
GOLD ON NAVY — 6.8:1 — PASSES
Legendary Dry
GOLD ON WHITE — 2.1:1 — FAILS

Make it a rule: gold on navy only. On light backgrounds set headings in navy and keep gold for rules, underlines and the filled button, where contrast is carried by the shape rather than the text.

05

Nothing on the homepage can be merchandised

The live site runs a nine-slide hero carousel — The Lady Legendary, the new 19.2 oz tall cans, Kingston Black, Beachcomber, Blue Skies, Happy Holidays. The redesign replaces all of it with one hard-coded Farmstand hero and a single “More Details” link. There is no surface left for a seasonal release or a new format, so the homepage will be out of date the week it launches. A carousel is not the only answer — a standing three-up promo rail under the hero is steadier and does the same job. But something has to hold that content.

06

The cider card introduces two off-brand colours

The palette is navy and gold. The card — the most repeated component on the site — adds a mint-green availability pill and a yellow ABV pill, neither of which appears anywhere else in the brand. The mint especially reads as a stock UI component rather than as Stormalong. Both badges can be navy outline and gold text on navy and lose nothing.

07

“We Respect The Apple” is 120 centred words on a busy pattern

Centred body copy is hard to read at any width because every line starts in a different place, and the damask pattern underneath costs further contrast. On a phone the section becomes roughly 700 px of near-empty navy before the button arrives. This is the brand's best line — give it a left-aligned two-column treatment, cut the copy to about half, and let “Respect the apple.” carry the weight as a pull quote.

Medium 7 findings · housekeeping

08 · Five font families, two unused

Inter, Oswald, Bebas Neue, Cinzel and Poppins all load on every page. Cinzel and Bebas Neue never render, and font-sans resolves to Poppins, so Inter is dead weight too. Drop to Oswald plus Poppins.

09 · Every heading shouts

A global rule sets h1–h6 to condensed uppercase. Nothing can be sentence case, so an h4 label competes with a page title and no level reads as more important than another. Scope it to h1–h2.

10 · The apple glyphs have no legend

A gold apple and a red apple sit under every cider detail headline with no label. Nobody can tell what they mean. Label them, or fold them into the spec list beside ABV and availability.

11 · Shares render blank

No OpenGraph or Twitter card images are set, so every link posted to Instagram, Facebook or a text message shows an empty box. The can artwork is the brand's strongest asset — it should be the share image on every cider page. The generator: 'v0.dev' tag is still in the metadata too.

12 · The newsletter form does nothing

The footer sign-up has no action and no submit handler. It collects an address, looks like it worked, and discards it. Either wire it to the mail provider or take it out.

13 · No age gate, no responsibility line

This is an alcohol brand with no age confirmation and no “drink responsibly” footer line. Worth a decision with whoever handles compliance rather than an oversight.

14 · The sweetness scale is under-labelled

Five segments, three labels. When the marker lands on segment 2 or 4 the reader has to interpolate. Name the active stop — “Dry”, “Semi-dry” — next to the bar. The component already knows the word.

Keep already better than the live site
  • The collection filters on /ciders — Core, Seasonal, Rare Apple, Barrel-Aged. The live site makes you scroll one undifferentiated list.
  • The sweetness scale. This is the question every cider shopper actually has, and no competitor answers it this plainly.
  • Previous / next arrows between cider pages — browsing the range without going back to the grid.
  • The awards block, pulled out of the body copy into its own panel. Eight GLINTCAP and SF Chronicle medals were buried before.
  • Type and spacing generally are calmer and more confident than stormalong.com.
Reviewed against stormalong.offthegully.com, the local build on localhost:3000, and the live stormalong.com, September 2026. Contrast figures are WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance ratios.
